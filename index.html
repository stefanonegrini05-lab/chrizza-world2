📚 A tiny grammar lesson

1. Essere — to be

Io sonoI am
Tu seiYou are
Lui/Lei èHe/She is
Noi siamoWe are
Voi sieteYou (plural) are
Loro sonoThey are
Example: Tu sei bellissima. — You are beautiful.

2. Avere — to have

Io hoI have
Tu haiYou have
Lui/Lei haHe/She has
Noi abbiamoWe have
Voi aveteYou (plural) have
Loro hannoThey have
Example: Ho fame! — I'm hungry! (Literally: I have hunger.)

3. Gender and endings

Many Italian nouns and adjectives ending in -o are masculine and those ending in -a are feminine. For example: bello / bella, carino / carina. Some words end in -e and can be either gender.

4. Making a sentence negative

Put non before the verb: Ti amo = I love you; Non ti amo = I don't love you. (But we definitely prefer the first one! 😋)

🗣️ Pronunciation tips

CH sounds like a hard K before E or I: Chi sounds like “kee”.
CI / CE sound like “chee / cheh”: Ciao is “chow”.
GN sounds similar to “ny” in canyon: lasagna.
GLI is a soft sound, roughly like “ly”: famiglia.
Double consonants are held a little longer: pala and palla sound different.
Vowels are clear and short: A = ah, E = eh, I = ee, O = oh, U = oo.

Little challenge: say “Ciao amore, mi manchi!” out loud. Stefano is your pronunciation judge. 😂
